# K-Pop Fan Psychology: Academic Research Deep Dive

Compiled from peer-reviewed journals including Frontiers in Psychology, SAGE Journals, Taylor & Francis, MDPI, ACM, Wiley, and Korean databases (KCI/RISS/DBpia).

## 1. Parasocial Relationships in K-Pop

### 1.1 Paper: "Positive Psychosocial Outcomes and Fanship in K-Pop Fans: A Social Identity Theory Perspective"
- **Authors:** Derek A. Laffan
- **Year:** 2021
- **Journal:** Psychological Reports (SAGE)
- **Sample:** 1,477 K-pop fans from 92 predominantly Western countries
- **Methodology:** Online survey measuring fanship, fandom, and psychosocial outcomes (happiness, self-esteem, social connectedness)
- **Key Findings:** K-pop fanship was a significant predictor of increased happiness, self-esteem, and social connectedness. All three psychosocial outcome models were statistically significant (p < .001 for happiness and self-esteem, p = .002 for social connectedness).
- **Killer stat:** 1,477 fans across 92 countries show K-pop fandom significantly predicts higher happiness, self-esteem, and social connectedness -- one of the largest empirical K-pop fan psychology studies to date.
- **Source:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0033294120961524

### 1.2 Paper: "Displays of Parasocial Interaction in K-Pop: A Content Analysis of YouTube Comments on BTS's Music Videos"
- **Authors:** Emily Flinchum, Enakshi Roy, Rauf Arif
- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal:** Korean Journal of Communication
- **Sample:** 1,114 fan comments on BTS YouTube videos
- **Methodology:** Content analysis coding for four PSI indicators: authenticity, affability, social attraction, fandom
- **Key Findings:** Approximately two-thirds (66%+) of analyzed comments displayed at least one parasocial interaction indicator. Social attraction was the most frequent indicator. Music videos showed higher affability and fandom; personality-driven nonmusic videos showed higher social attraction.
- **Killer stat:** Two-thirds of all fan comments on BTS videos display measurable parasocial interaction indicators -- fans aren't just watching, they're actively relating.
- **Source:** https://muse.jhu.edu/pub/15/article/932421

### 1.3 Paper: "From Parasocial to Parakin: Co-creating Idols on Social Media"
- **Authors:** Qing Yan, Fan Yang
- **Year:** 2021
- **Journal:** New Media & Society (SAGE), Vol. 23(9), pp. 2593-2615
- **Methodology:** Grounded theory approach using text analysis and in-depth interviews with Chinese fans
- **Key Findings:** A new idol-fan relationship model has emerged where fans go beyond worship to co-cultivate their idols' public images, career scope, and personal lives. Proposed a dual motivation model: fans use the internet to generate traffic and drive long-term strategic plans for idols. Coined the term "parakin" -- fans as active co-creators, not passive consumers.
- **Killer stat:** K-pop fan relationships have evolved from "parasocial" to "parakin" -- fans don't just admire idols, they co-create them.
- **Source:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1461444820933313

### 1.4 Paper: "Parasocial Media: The Mass Production of Intimacy on a Chinese Pop Idol Mobile Application"
- **Authors:** Xinyue Shen, Blake Hallinan
- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal:** Platforms & Society (SAGE)
- **Methodology:** Case study of Pocket48, the custom mobile app for idol group SNH48
- **Key Findings:** Defined "parasocial media" as platforms with (1) asymmetrical user relationships, (2) tiered access to content/communication, and (3) tools to directly monetize relationships. The app employs "underclassifying interactions" and "invoking the aesthetics of intimacy" to channel fan-idol relationships while unobtrusively incorporating transactions.
- **Killer stat:** Platforms like Bubble and Pocket48 are purpose-built "parasocial media" -- designed to mass-produce the feeling of intimacy at scale while monetizing it.
- **Source:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/29768624241299954

### 1.5 Paper: "Can Fans Build Parasocial Relationships through Idols' Simulated Voice Messages?"
- **Authors:** Haesoo Kim et al. (KAIST)
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction
- **Sample:** 15 fans who used "AI Private Call" (AI-generated idol voice message service)
- **Methodology:** Semi-structured interviews
- **Key Findings:** AI synthetic voices alone could not provide impressive idol experiences. However, fans engaged in dynamic cognitive and behavioral actions to advance the relationship: aligning voice content with idols' vocal characteristics, adhering to social norms in interactions, and confirming idols' involvement to maintain a sense of authenticity. Fandom communities significantly affected experiences throughout the process.
- **Killer stat:** Even with AI-generated voice messages, fans actively worked to construct parasocial relationships -- confirming that fans are co-creators of intimacy, not passive recipients.
- **Source:** https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3711111


## 2. Fan Co-Creation and the "National Producer" Phenomenon

### 2.1 Paper: "When Young Female Fans Were Producing Celebrities: The Influential Factors Related to Online Fan Engagement, Celebrity Worship, and Program Commitment in Idol-Nurturing Reality Shows"
- **Authors:** Lan Zhang et al.
- **Year:** 2022
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Communication
- **Sample:** 3,352 young Chinese respondents
- **Methodology:** Survey measuring online fan engagement, celebrity worship, and program commitment across 11 idol-nurturing reality shows (Produce 101, Produce 48, Produce X 101, Youth With You, CHUANG, etc.)
- **Key Findings:** Celebrity worship significantly and positively correlates with program commitment and mediates the relationship between online fan engagement and program commitment. 67.4% of respondents watched Youth With You S2; 59.2% watched Produce 101 China. The audience-driven concept develops deep emotional connections, making fans willing to participate in engagement activities.
- **Killer stat:** In a sample of 3,352 fans, celebrity worship mediated the path from online engagement to program commitment -- the more fans worship, the more they commit to "producing" their idol.
- **Source:** https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2022.931185/full

### 2.2 Paper: "K-pop Fan Labor and an Alternative Creative Industry: A Case Study of GOT7 Chinese Fans"
- **Authors:** Meicheng Sun
- **Year:** 2020
- **Journal:** Global Media and China (SAGE)
- **Methodology:** Participant observation during GOT7 events in Chinese cities and Korea (September-December 2019)
- **Key Findings:** Identified three categories of K-pop fan labor: specialized labor (translations, creative content), managerial labor (organizing fan clubs), and unskilled labor (mass streaming, voting). Fan translation work ranges from a few minutes to 10+ hours on a given day. Fans perceive themselves as consumers who "repay" the industry, yet this "repaying" is a critical step in the production and distribution process.
- **Killer stat:** Fan translators dedicate anywhere from minutes to 10+ hours per day -- unpaid labor that is "indispensable" to the K-pop industry's global operation.
- **Source:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2059436420954588

### 2.3 Documented Case: Cai Xukun Voting on "Idol Producer" (2018)
- **Context:** Chinese idol-nurturing reality show based on Produce 101 format
- **Key Data:** Fans cast 47.64 million votes for Cai Xukun in only 12 days. The show received over 100 million total votes. As paid voting evolved, fans organized crowdfunding campaigns; fans publicly declared willingness to pay 300 million yuan (47+ million USD) in legal costs for their idol. Forbes ranked him 31st in 2020 China Celebrity 100.
- **Killer stat:** 47.64 million votes in 12 days -- when fans become "national producers," their investment becomes unlimited.
- **Sources:** https://pressbooks.lib.vt.edu/pper/chapter/article-3/ and https://pandaily.com/idol-producer-over-100-million-votes-casted-for-chinas-next-boy-group/

### 2.4 Paper: "Affective Participation From the In-Between: The Platformization of K-Pop Fandom"
- **Authors:** Samantha James
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** Social Media + Society (SAGE)
- **Methodology:** 10 months of ethnographic fieldwork (2022-2023)
- **Key Findings:** Fans simultaneously subvert and support industry expectations. Navigation practices include gatekeeping insider knowledge, community policing, and self-cooptation. Although fans creatively subvert systems of power, they reinvent structures that ultimately benefit K-pop industry leaders and platform owners.
- **Killer stat:** 10 months of fieldwork reveals K-pop fans exist "in-between" -- simultaneously co-creators and exploited laborers within the platform ecosystem.
- **Source:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20563051251351390


## 3. Psychological Ownership in Fandom

### 3.1 Paper: "Formation of Fan Psychological Ownership and Its Influence on Fan Enthusiasm: A Hybrid Approach Using PLS-SEM and fsQCA"
- **Authors:** Multiple (Chinese researchers)
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Psychology
- **Sample:** 402 Chinese fans of fostered idols (Teens in Times and TFBOYS)
- **Methodology:** PLS-SEM combined with fuzzy-set Qualitative Comparative Analysis (fsQCA)
- **Key Findings:** Proposed a dual-pathway model: (1) idol-fan psychological ownership and (2) fans' collective psychological ownership. Self-investment, intimate interaction, and cultural symbol value all positively contribute to both forms of ownership. Three distinct configurations lead to high fan enthusiasm: interaction-oriented, control-oriented, and emotion-driven patterns. Both forms of psychological ownership significantly enhance fan enthusiasm.
- **Killer stat:** Self-investment, intimate interaction, and cultural symbol value ALL drive psychological ownership -- and that ownership directly fuels fan enthusiasm. Fans who feel they "own" a piece of the idol invest more.
- **Source:** https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1721382/full

### 3.2 Paper: "Breaking Up With My Idol: A Qualitative Study of the Psychological Adaptation Process of Renouncing Fanship"
- **Authors:** Multiple
- **Year:** 2022
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Psychology
- **Methodology:** Netnography across Weibo, WeChat, and Douban over 3 years
- **Key Findings:** Renouncing an idol follows three phases: resistance (acute stress), negotiation (bargaining), and recovery (attachment reconstruction). Fans face four barriers to leaving: cognitive dissonance, emotional attachment, behavioral dependence, and social threat. Fans share trauma with fellow renouncing fans to obtain social identity for leaving and relieve distress.
- **Killer stat:** Breaking up with an idol is psychologically comparable to ending a real relationship -- fans experience acute stress, cognitive dissonance, and social threat when trying to leave.
- **Source:** https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.1030470/full

### 3.3 Paper: "Idol Worship: How Does It Influence Fan Consumers' Brand Loyalty?"
- **Authors:** Multiple
- **Year:** 2022
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Psychology
- **Sample:** 332 valid questionnaires (from 388 distributed)
- **Methodology:** Structural equation modeling with mediation analysis
- **Key Findings:** Identified four drivers of idol-linked brand loyalty: brand personality appeal, perceived emotional value, brand-based self-realization, and relatedness needs satisfaction. Brand passion directly affects brand loyalty AND indirectly through brand attachment. Fans buy products to display love and imitate idol behavior to achieve their "ideal selves."
- **Killer stat:** Fans buy endorsed products not just for the product -- but to achieve their "ideal selves" by imitating their idol, turning consumption into identity construction.
- **Source:** https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.850670/full


## 4. K-Pop Fan Spending and Economics

### 4.1 Korea Foundation Global Hallyu Survey (2023)
- **Survey scope:** 12,566 respondents across 26 countries
- **Key Data:**
  - Global average K-pop spending: $4.50/month (down from ~$7.00 the previous year)
  - UAE fans: $17.60/month (highest)
  - US & Saudi Arabia: ~$8.00/month each
  - US K-pop fans spend $24/month on merchandise -- 2.4x higher than average American music listener
  - US K-pop fans are 50% more likely to purchase merchandise even when unable to attend events
  - Thailand and India fans: ~21 hours/month on K-pop content
  - Vietnam: ~20 hours/month; Indonesia: ~19 hours/month
  - 225 million Hallyu fans worldwide (up from 9.26M in 2012 -- a 24x increase)
- **Killer stat:** 225 million global fans, 24x growth in a decade -- and the most dedicated spend $24/month on merchandise alone.
- **Source:** https://issuu.com/the_korea_foundation/docs/0402_global_hallyu_status_eng

### 4.2 Superfan Economics: Goldman Sachs & Luminate Data
- **Source:** Goldman Sachs "Music in the Air" Report (2024) and Luminate surveys
- **Key Data:**
  - Luminate: 19% of US music listeners qualify as "superfans" (engage in 5+ ways)
  - Goldman Sachs: assumes 20% of paid streaming subscribers are superfans
  - Superfans spend 80% more per month than average music fans
  - Superfans spend 66% more on live music, 2x as much on physical purchases
  - Goldman Sachs estimates a $4.5 billion global superfan opportunity
  - Projected $3.3 billion incremental revenue (13% uplift to paid streaming) by 2030
  - MIDiA/SoundCloud: 1.9% of listeners generating 42% of total revenue under fan-powered royalties
- **Killer stat:** 1.9% of listeners generate 42% of revenue -- the superfan economy is a power law, and K-pop pioneered it.
- **Source:** https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/emerging-markets-superfans-and-price-rises-7-takeaways-from-goldman-sachs-new-music-in-the-air-report/

### 4.3 iPrice K-Pop Fan Spending Study (cited in Vice)
- **Methodology:** E-commerce data aggregated from hundreds of merchants in Southeast Asia and Hong Kong
- **Key Data:**
  - BTS fans (ARMY): ~$1,422 total (merchandise + 15 albums/EPs + 5 concerts)
  - TWICE fans (Once): ~$824 total (merchandise + 14 albums + 4 concerts)
  - BLACKPINK fans (Blinks): ~$665 total (merchandise + 5 albums/EPs + concerts)
  - Self-reported Reddit surveys show $150-$400/month for dedicated fans
- **Killer stat:** A single BTS fan can easily spend $1,422+ over the lifecycle of their fandom -- and that's the moderate estimate.
- **Source:** https://www.vice.com/en/article/kpop-fans-money-spent-merchandise-albums-concerts-korea-bts-bt21-blackpink/


## 5. K-Pop as Emotional Regulation and Coping

### 5.1 Paper: "The Mediating Effect of Social Connectedness in the Relationship Between K-Pop Fandom Identity and Mental Health"
- **Authors:** Jerizza Joy R. Arizabal, Homer J. Yabut
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** SAGE Open
- **Methodology:** Survey measuring K-pop fandom identity, social connectedness, subjective well-being, and depression
- **Key Findings:** Social connectedness fully mediated the relationship between K-pop fandom identity and subjective well-being. K-pop fandom identity also directly predicted lower levels of depression (without mediation through social connectedness). The path is: fandom identity -> social connections -> well-being.
- **Killer stat:** K-pop fandom identity directly predicts lower depression levels, and its effect on well-being is fully mediated by social connectedness -- being a fan makes you feel less alone.
- **Source:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/21582440251369989

### 5.2 Paper: "Escapism and Motivation: Understanding K-pop Fans Well-being and Identity"
- **Authors:** Nur Ayuni Mohd Jenol, Nur Hafeeza Ahmad Pazil
- **Year:** 2020
- **Journal:** Geografia: Malaysian Journal of Society and Space, Vol. 16(4), pp. 336-347
- **Sample:** 8 participants (qualitative)
- **Methodology:** Participant observation at K-pop concerts/fan events + semi-structured in-depth interviews
- **Key Findings:** Being a K-pop fan provides a platform for escapism, emotional support, and motivational reinforcement through parasocial relationships. Fans are active producers and content creators, contradicting the notion of fans as passive entities.
- **Killer stat:** K-pop fandom transcends music genre -- it serves as an active coping mechanism providing escapism, emotional regulation, and motivation.
- **Source:** https://www.researchgate.net/publication/346604107_Escapism_and_Motivation_Understanding_K-pop_Fans_Well-being_and_Identity

### 5.3 Paper: "K-pop Fandom's Affective Role in Shaping Knowledge of Gender and Sexuality among LGBTQ+ Fans in Australia and the Philippines"
- **Authors:** Thomas Baudinette, Kelsey E. Scholes
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** Sexualities (SAGE)
- **Sample:** 17 LGBTQ+ K-pop fans from Australia and the Philippines
- **Methodology:** Qualitative interviews
- **Key Findings:** K-pop idols' playful gendered performances are important sources of queer knowledge for fans identifying as non-binary, genderqueer, or trans. K-pop fandom spaces on social media were considered safe environments supporting LGBTQ+ fans' identity exploration. K-pop was celebrated for its normalization of androgynous styling and performative challenge to stereotypical gender roles.
- **Killer stat:** K-pop fandom is emerging as a significant safe space for LGBTQ+ identity exploration -- idols' androgynous performances serve as "queer knowledge" sources.
- **Source:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/13634607241275855


## 6. Virtual Idol Attachment

### 6.1 Paper: "Virtual Presence, Real Connections: Exploring the Role of Parasocial Relationships in Virtual Idol Fan Community Participation"
- **Authors:** Jiahui Liu
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** Global Media and China (SAGE)
- **Methodology:** Survey examining A-SOUL (pioneering Chinese virtual idol group)
- **Key Findings:** Both perceived interpersonal attractiveness of virtual idols and fans' loneliness significantly influence the development of parasocial relationships. PSRs positively impact fan community participation. Loneliness and attractiveness directly contribute to increased participation through the mediating effect of PSRs. Provides evidence for the "de-stigmatization" of subcultures in the AI era.
- **Killer stat:** Loneliness is a direct driver of virtual idol parasocial relationships -- the lonelier the fan, the stronger the bond with virtual characters.
- **Source:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20594364231222976

### 6.2 Paper: "The Creative Behavior of Virtual Idol Fans: A Psychological Perspective Based on MOA Theory"
- **Authors:** Multiple
- **Year:** 2023
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Psychology
- **Methodology:** Questionnaire-based survey using Motivation-Opportunity-Ability (MOA) theory combined with self-determination theory
- **Key Findings:** Interest motivation, achievement motivation, social motivation, and utility motivation all positively influence fans' creative behavior. Fans' perceived cost had a significant negative moderating effect -- when fans perceive creation as "expensive" (time/effort), interest, social, and utility motivations weaken. The virtual idol industry should focus on reducing perceived costs of fan creation.
- **Killer stat:** Four distinct motivations drive fans to CREATE for virtual idols -- but perceived cost dampens all of them. Lower the barrier to create, and fans will flood the ecosystem.
- **Source:** https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1290790/full

### 6.3 Paper: "I Stan Alien Idols and Also the People Behind Them: Understanding How Seams Between Virtual and Real Identities Engage VTuber Fans -- A Case Study of PLAVE"
- **Authors:** Multiple (Korean researchers)
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (ACM)
- **Sample:** 24 fans of PLAVE (Korean virtual K-pop idol group)
- **Methodology:** Semi-structured interviews
- **Key Findings:** Identified two sources of "seams" (moments where virtual/real blur): technical glitches and identity collapses. More than half of participants integrated both virtual and real aspects into a cohesive understanding of PLAVE. Others deliberately distanced from real identities. These seamful moments sparked curiosity and deepened engagement rather than breaking immersion.
- **Killer stat:** When virtual idol facades "break," fans don't disengage -- over half actively integrate the glitch into a deeper, more authentic connection.
- **Source:** https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3706598.3714218


## 7. The IKEA Effect and Effort Justification in Entertainment

### 7.1 Paper: "The IKEA Effect: When Labor Leads to Love"
- **Authors:** Michael I. Norton, Daniel Mochon, Dan Ariely
- **Year:** 2012
- **Journal:** Journal of Consumer Psychology, Vol. 22(3), pp. 453-460
- **Methodology:** Four experiments (IKEA box assembly, origami folding, LEGO building)
- **Key Findings:** Participants valued self-assembled products significantly more than identical pre-assembled ones. They saw their amateurish creations as similar in value to expert creations and expected others to share their opinions. Critical boundary condition: the effect only occurs when labor results in successful completion. Participants expressed desire to show creations to friends (social utility component).
- **Killer stat:** People value their own amateurish creations as highly as expert work -- but only when they complete the task. Successful creation = love.
- **Source:** https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/11-091.pdf

### 7.2 Paper: "Character Customization With Cosmetic Microtransactions in Games: Subjective Experience and Objective Performance"
- **Authors:** Multiple
- **Year:** 2022
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Psychology
- **Methodology:** Laboratory experiment using League of Legends
- **Key Findings:** Customization increased subjective identification with the player character. Identification was positively related to perceived competence, fun, and self-estimated performance (though objective performance was unaffected). Identification positively affects players' intention to make microtransactions.
- **Killer stat:** Character customization increases identification, which increases fun, perceived competence, AND spending intention -- the self-expression-to-monetization pipeline.
- **Source:** https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.770139/full

### 7.3 Paper: "What Factors Affect Psychological Ownership When Creating an Avatar?: Focusing on Customization and the Ideal Self"
- **Authors:** Kim et al.
- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal:** Telematics and Informatics (ScienceDirect)
- **Methodology:** 2x2 experimental design (personalization vs. customization) x (ideal self vs. actual self)
- **Key Findings:** Psychological ownership of avatar was highest when customization was combined with actual self-representation. There was a direct effect on psychological ownership only when avatars were customized (not merely personalized). A positive mediating effect was found where customization affects psychological ownership via attachment.
- **Killer stat:** Customization + actual self = highest psychological ownership. When users shape a character that feels like "them," they own it psychologically.
- **Source:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0736585324000029

### 7.4 Paper: "Me, My Avatar, and My Sneakers: The Effect of Avatar Customization on the Psychological Ownership of Virtual Fashion Items"
- **Authors:** Multiple
- **Year:** 2025
- **Journal:** Computers in Human Behavior (ScienceDirect)
- **Methodology:** Serial mediation analysis
- **Key Findings:** Avatar customization enhanced avatar embodiment, resulting in greater psychological ownership and purchase intention for virtual fashion items. The effect is moderated by avatar human likeness. Industry context: 95% of consumers are interested in purchasing digital fashion for avatars; 82% have already done so.
- **Killer stat:** 95% of consumers want to buy digital fashion for their avatars, and customization is the gateway -- it drives embodiment -> ownership -> purchase intent.
- **Source:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563225001001


## 8. Identity Construction Through K-Pop Fandom

### 8.1 Paper: "Celebrity Worship and Body Image Among Young Girls Fans of K-Pop Girl Groups"
- **Authors:** Multiple (Indonesian researchers)
- **Year:** 2021
- **Journal:** HUMANITAS: Indonesian Psychological Journal
- **Sample:** 414 female adolescents aged 18-22
- **Methodology:** Celebrity Attitude Scale (CAS) + Multidimensional Body-Self Relation Questionnaire-Appearance Scale (MBSRQ-AS)
- **Key Findings:** Entertainment-social worship was the highest-scoring dimension; borderline-pathological was the lowest. The entertainment-social aspect showed significant positive correlation with appearance orientation. Fans orient their body image and appearance standards based on idol worship.
- **Killer stat:** Among 414 young female fans, entertainment-social celebrity worship significantly correlated with appearance orientation -- fans actively reshape their physical identity to match their idols.
- **Source:** https://journal.uad.ac.id/index.php/HUMANITAS/article/view/19392

### 8.2 Paper: "Between Universes: Fan Positionalities in the Transnational Circulation of K-Pop"
- **Authors:** Kyong Yoon
- **Year:** 2022
- **Journal:** Communication and the Public (SAGE), Vol. 7(4), pp. 188-201
- **Methodology:** Qualitative interviews with K-pop fans in three Canadian cities (Toronto, Vancouver, Kelowna) from 2015-2021
- **Key Findings:** Transnational fans are not a homogeneous group; they negotiate their own positionalities (race, gender) when engaging with K-pop. White and Asian fans experience K-pop differently in multi-ethnic Western contexts. K-pop's meaning is "not yet clearly situated" in local Western contexts, leaving room for diverse identity negotiation.
- **Killer stat:** K-pop's cultural ambiguity is a feature, not a bug -- fans of different racial backgrounds project different meanings onto the same content, making it universally adoptable.
- **Source:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/20570473221136667

### 8.3 Korean Academic Study (KCI/DBpia): Generational Fandom Culture Shift
- **Source:** KCI (Korea Citation Index), published in Korean
- **Key Finding:** 3rd generation idol fandom (BTS/BLACKPINK era) showed higher intimacy, romantic/worship psychology, and perceived similarity toward idols compared to 1st and 2nd generation fandoms. The shift correlates with social media enabling constant parasocial contact.
- **Source:** https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART002733238

### 8.4 Korean Academic Study (RISS): Adult Women's Idol Group Fan Experience
- **Source:** RISS (Research Information Sharing Service), Master's thesis
- **Key Finding:** Adult women fans reduce stress and treat idol groups as vitality sources or positive stimuli. They discover meaning in fan experiences through expanding friendships and self-development. Contradicts the stereotype that fandom is only for teenagers.
- **Source:** https://m.riss.kr/link?id=T13247257
